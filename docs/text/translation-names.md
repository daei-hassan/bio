# Names and terms for chapters 2–8

Fixed spellings for the English and European Portuguese translations of
`docs/text/fa/zendegi-nameh-final.md`, lines 115–1058. This list continues
`docs/text/translation-notes.md` (front matter and chapter 1) and does not repeat
what is already fixed there, except where a later chapter uses a new Persian form.

**How to read the tables**

- "First line" is the line number in the Persian file where the item first appears
  in chapters 2–8.
- "same" in the Portuguese column means the name is spelled exactly as in English.
  Only the title changes: Mr. → o Sr., Ms./Mrs. → a Sr.ª, Dr. → o Dr. / a Dr.ª,
  Professor → o professor, "the late" → o falecido / a falecida.
- Chapter boundaries: ch. 2 = 115–262, ch. 3 = 263–417, ch. 4 = 418–446,
  ch. 5 = 447–589, ch. 6 = 590–617, ch. 7 = 618–695, ch. 8 = 696–1058.

**Spelling choices made here, following chapter 1**

- Long u is `u`, long i is `i`, ق is `gh`, ع is dropped: Mahmud, Davud, Naser,
  Ghavami, Said (like Moin, Esmail, Masumeh, Sadeghi).
- Given names written as one word in Persian but made of two names take a hyphen,
  like Mohammad-Hadi: Ali-Reza, Gholam-Reza, Rajab-Ali, Mohammad-Amin.
- Compound surnames are one word (Roshanruz, Esmailpur, Nuralizadeh, Zarrabvand);
  a hyphen is used only where two full names are joined (Fallah-Moghaddam,
  Haj-Taheri, Ghods-Ardabili, Ghazi-Mirsaid).
- آقای + surname is "Mr." (chapter 1: Mr. Samavati). By the same logic خانم +
  surname is "Ms." here (see open question 30). Given name + آقا / خانم keeps the
  Persian title: Asghar Agha, Soheila Khanom, Hasan Agha.
- Public figures keep their established spelling even where it breaks these rules
  (Farrokhroo Parsa, Mousavi, Nateq-Nouri, Massoud Rajavi, Sadeq Chubak).

## 2. Places

Generic words: خیابان = Street / Rua (Avenida for the great avenues), میدان =
Square / Praça, کوچه = Lane / Travessa, چهارراه = crossroads / cruzamento,
محله = neighbourhood / bairro, استان = Province / província, پل = Bridge / Ponte.

Tehran's numbered districts need two words, because line 227 sets one against the
other: ناحیه (the education districts before the revolution) is "Education Area" /
"Zona Escolar"; منطقه (today's districts) is "District" / "Distrito". So
ناحیه‌ی سیزده = Education Area 13, منطقه‌ی هفده = District 17. The same pattern
serves Areas 1, 2, 5, 7 and 11 and Districts 3, 10, 11 and 17.

Country names in Portuguese are the European forms: Irão, Iraque, Japão, Suíça,
Alemanha, França, Espanha, Itália, Brasil, Canadá, Austrália.

## 8. Dates

Renderings follow the date table in `translation-notes.md`. The Portuguese pattern is
the same with "de": `25 de Tir de 1339 (16 de julho de 1960)`. Two-digit years are
written in full. Square brackets mark a year the text leaves out; the translator may
supply the Gregorian year in the bracket only. Every full date was computed, not
looked up. Nowruz fell on 21 March in all the years concerned except 1375 (20 March
1996) and 1379 (20 March 2000).

Dates the source already gives in the Gregorian calendar are left alone: «مدل ۱۹۹۰»
(848), «بیست‌وپنجم آوریل» (858), «اوایل قرن بیستم» (942), «۱۳ ماه مه» (944, 952),
«۲۰۰۵ میلادی» (948). Lunar dates stay as written: «سی‌ام رمضان» (151), Ramadan and
the Nights of Qadr (856, 860; Ramadan fell in January 1998), mid-Shaban (878).

## 9. Open questions

Readings that the Persian does not settle. The spelling used in the tables above is
given first.

1. **بیگلری (167) and بهمن بیگلر (171 onwards).** Biglari on the class register,
   Bahman Biglar everywhere after. One boy or two? If one, which surname?
2. **آقای علیزاده (169) and آقای نورعلی‌زاده (199, 203, 207).** Mr. Alizadeh teaches
   mathematics in the first year, Mr. Nuralizadeh in the second, and line 185 says the
   teachers were "almost the same". One man or two?
3. **آقای گل‌نهایی (145, 147).** Golnahayi as written. It could be read Gol-Nehayi,
   or be a slip for گل‌نهالی (Golnahali).
4. **خانم سراج (213).** Seraj or Sarraj.
5. **ژوزف انویه (275).** Joseph Anvieh. The surname of this Christian classmate has no
   vowels marked: Anvieh, Enviyeh or Anuyeh. Also Joseph or Zhozef for the first name.
6. **داوود جلولی (275 and nine more places).** Jaluli, Jeloli or Jalvali. He is in
   chapters 3 to 7, so this one has to be settled before translation starts.
7. **جلال قربی (275).** Ghorbi or Ghorabi.
8. **آقای انبه (277, 279).** Anbeh as written (it is also the word for mango); perhaps
   Anbah or Onbeh.
9. **آقای ذلیکانی (279).** Zalikani, Zolikani or Zeleykani.
10. **حمید بلالی (307, 422, 428).** Belali or Balali.
11. **صالحیان (502) and صالح جلالیان (506).** Both are the third science teacher on the
    same shift beside Salari. Salehian and Saleh Jalalian: one man or two?
12. **Pupils at line 508.** باخلاق: Bakhlagh as written, or a slip for بااخلاق
    (Ba-akhlagh). جریانی: Jaryani or Jarayani.
13. **آقای تمسکی (538, 544, 550).** Tamassoki, Tamaski or Temeski.
14. **آقای میرباباپی (664).** Mirbabapi as written; probably a typing slip for
    میربابایی (Mirbabayi).
15. **حمید افشار (666) and آقای افشاری, "two brothers named Afshari" (924).** Afshar
    and Afshari: the same family or not?
16. **علیم (798) and علیمو (966).** The embassy driver is Alim at first and Alimo
    later. Which is his name, and how did he spell it in Portuguese?
17. **آقای دروچی (814, 846, 880, 882).** Daruchi, Doruchi or Dorochi.
18. **آقای گوهرپی (880 and five more places).** Goharpey or Goharpi.
19. **آقای آیرم (968, 994, 1034, 1036).** Ayrom, Ayrum or Airam.
20. **Spellings that the people themselves may have fixed.** Maryam, Maedeh and
    Mahrokh (their passports), and Dr. Parviz Meykadeh, who practised in Cascais
    and will have had a Latin spelling there (Meykadeh, Maykadeh, Meikadeh).
21. **وصفنار (500, 578).** Vasfenar as the author writes it. The neighbourhood's
    usual written name is وصفنارد (Vasfenard). Keep his form or the map's?
22. **کوی طالب (524).** Kuy-e Taleb as written. The old Mashhad quarter is کوی طلاب
    (Kuy-e Tollab). A slip?
23. **آویرا (860).** The poor neighbourhood where the Pakistani Shia had their hall.
    I could not match "Avira" to a Lisbon neighbourhood, and it is not Aveiro. What
    is its Portuguese name?
24. **کاظم (826).** Listed with Fátima, Alzira and Algés as an Arabic survival in
    Portuguese. Probably the place-name Cacém, heard as Kazem; not confirmed.
25. **شهر آلکاسر (908).** The small town "near Madrid" where they slept on the way
    back by the national roads. Cáceres, Alcázar de San Juan and Alcalá are all
    possible; the author himself says "if I am not mistaken".
26. **میدان پلازا (906).** "Plaza Square" in Madrid: Plaza Mayor, or another?
27. **خیابان سعدی (1038).** A narrow "Saadi Street" behind the Champs-Élysées. I know
    of no street of that name there. Is it the real name, or how the Iranians called it?
28. **«عظمت بازیافته» "by Mohammad Reza Pahlavi" (207).** The Shah's known books have
    other titles. Is the title, or the author, misremembered?
29. **کتاب «گایا» (974).** "Gaia" or "Gaya" as written, the yearbook of the
    Portuguese Ministry of Education. It may be the Portuguese word *guia* (guide).
30. **Conventions chosen here that an editor may want to overrule.** خانم + surname
    as "Ms." (Mrs. only for Soheila's mother); سعید as Said rather than Saeed;
    علیرضا and غلامرضا as Ali-Reza and Gholam-Reza rather than Alireza and Gholamreza;
    ناحیه as "Education Area" against منطقه as "District".
31. **«داکولیت‌ها، باکولیت‌ها» (490).** The geological terms would be لاکولیت
    (laccoliths) and باتولیت (batholiths). Is the garbling part of the students' joke,
    or a slip? As written they are "dacoliths, bacoliths".
32. **Smaller readings, one occurrence each.** قدس اردبیلی (223): one double surname,
    Ghods-Ardabili, or given name and surname. محمد رشید فلاحی (293): Mohammad Rashid
    Fallahi, or surname Rashid-Fallahi. صنوبری (459): Senobari or Sanobari. رثایی (471):
    Resayi or Rasayi. خدری (496, 540): Khedri or Khodri. خشتیان (500): Kheshtian or
    Khashtian. شعیبی (502): Shoeibi or Shoaybi. مه‌آبادی (540): Mahabadi or Mehabadi.
