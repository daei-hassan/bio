# Translation notes

Rules and fixed spellings for the English and Portuguese translations of the
memoir. Both translations follow this file. The site build does not read it.

The lists in this file cover the front matter and chapter 1. Everything named
in chapters 2 to 8 (people, places, institutions, terms, titles, money, verse
and every date) is in `translation-names.md`, beside this file, in both
languages. Its last section lists the names whose reading is uncertain.

## Rules

- **Paragraph for paragraph.** One translated paragraph for each Persian
  paragraph. Nothing is cut, merged, summarised or embellished.
- **Voice.** First person, plain, the author's own register. His ellipses (…),
  asides in brackets and exclamations are kept.
- **Markup.** The same as the Persian file: `## Label: Title` for a chapter,
  `### …` for a section, `*…*<br>` for a line of verse, `**bold**`, `*italic*`.
- **Spelling (English).** Canadian: neighbour, metre, realize, diaper.
- **Nothing added.** A short gloss of a Persian word is allowed. Facts,
  names or explanations the author did not give are not, and his own
  factual slips are translated as written (they are listed at the end).
- **Portuguese** is European Portuguese and is translated from the Persian, not
  from the English. See "Portuguese" below.

### Dates

| Persian text | English |
|---|---|
| Full date | Solar Hijri date, then the Gregorian in brackets: `25 Tir 1339 (16 July 1960)` |
| Month and year | `Mehr 1348 (autumn 1969)` |
| Year only | `1346 (1967–68)`, since the Persian year runs from March to March |
| Year with a season or other clue | The single Gregorian year: `spring of 1347 (1968)` |
| Two-digit year (`سال ۴۶`) | Written in full: `1346` |
| Month alone, where the season matters | `Azar (early December)` |
| Lunar date | Left as written: `21 Muharram 1380` |
| Years on the cover | Gregorian only: `1960 – 2000` |

A date already converted earlier in the same passage is not converted again.

### Names and titles

- Plain transliteration, no accents or diacritics.
- Titles that are part of a name stay in Persian and are not explained:
  Seyed, Haj, Karbalayi, Khanom, Agha.
- Family words used as names get one short gloss at first use, then stand
  alone: Dadash (brother), Abji (older sister), Dayi (mother's brother),
  Amu (father's brother, also a respectful name for a neighbour).
- Persian says which side of the family a relative is on. The translation
  keeps that: "a cousin on my father's side", not "a cousin".

### Persian words kept in the text

Set in italics, with a short gloss inside the sentence the first time. No
footnotes. Where a joke or a nickname depends on something every Persian
reader knows, one short clause may explain it (so far only *ab, baba* in
chapter 1).

## Portuguese

European Portuguese, in the current spelling (ação, diretor, receção; facto
and carácter keep their c).

- **Voice.** As in English: first person, plain. European usage throughout:
  "estava a brincar", "a minha mãe", "casa de banho", "autocarro", "camião",
  "miúdos", "pequeno-almoço", "telemóvel".
- **Quotation marks.** «…» for speech and quoted words, as in the Persian.
  “…” only for a quotation inside one.
- **Dates.** `25 de Tir de 1339 (16 de julho de 1960)`, `Mehr de 1348 (outono
  de 1969)`, `1346 (1967–68)`. Gregorian month names in lower case.
- **People.** Spelled exactly as in the English column of this file.
- **Places.** The established Portuguese name where there is one, otherwise
  the English spelling: Teerão, Hamadã, Isfahão, Genebra, Lisboa, o Tejo.
  Places in Portugal get their real Portuguese names and spelling.
- **Persian words kept in the text.** The same transliteration as English, in
  italics, with a short Portuguese gloss the first time.

## Questions for the author

1. **Hasan or Hassan?** The README says "Seyed Hasan Jafari"; the repository
   is named `daei-hassan`. The translation uses Hasan.
2. **سید معین مهاجری.** Translated as a name, "Seyed Moin Mohajeri, who had
   made that journey". The Persian can equally be read "Seyed Moin, a migrant
   who had made that journey". Which is it?
3. **آسید.** Kept as "Aseyed" where the author writes it that way. Should it
   be "Seyed" throughout instead?
4. **Three places in chapter 1 where the Persian seems to disagree with itself.** They are
   translated as written.
   - The truck driver married Robab "a few years later", but the pump came in
     1346 and her marriage is dated 1347.
   - "A child of ten" at Father's death in Azar 1350; by the true birth date
     the author was eleven.
   - "That cold winter day": 23 Azar (14 December) is still autumn.
5. **Names in chapters 2 to 8 whose reading is uncertain.** Section 9 of
   `translation-names.md` lists them, with the spelling used and the
   alternatives.

## The revised edition of 7 October 2026

The author's revised PDF (93 pages, A4) replaced the first edition (204 pages,
A5). The Persian Markdown was brought into line with it, and the translations
were updated wherever the meaning changed.

- **What changed in the Persian.** About 370 small changes in 206 paragraphs:
  mostly spelling, word forms and spacing (ماهیانه for ماهانه, مسئولین for
  مسئولان, به راحتی for به‌راحتی), about 35 changes of meaning, a new
  dedication, one added clause in chapter 8 (line 796) and one partly
  rewritten paragraph (line 966). No heading or paragraph was added or removed.
- **How.** There is no text file behind the PDF, so its text was read out of
  the PDF and compared with the Markdown word by word. Every difference was
  either applied or set aside as a reading artefact.
- **Followed exactly:** the author's words, spellings, word spacing and
  punctuation, including Latin digits where he typed them (lines 966, 1014).
- **Not followed:** three dots for the ellipsis character (line 606), straight
  quotation marks for «…» (line 638), and stray spaces inside brackets. These
  are typing habits, not wording.
- **Could not be read reliably from the PDF:** vowel marks (the kasra of the
  ezafe, tanvin, tashdid). Where a word is otherwise unchanged its marks were
  kept as they were.
- **Line numbers.** The dedication is one line now instead of four. Three blank
  lines were left after it so that every later line keeps the number this file
  and `translation-names.md` refer to. Do not remove them.

## Places where the Persian seems to slip

Found while translating and second-reading. Line numbers are in
`docs/text/fa/zendegi-nameh-final.md`. Each is translated as written in both
languages. If the author corrects the Persian, make the same correction in
both translations.

### Chapter 2

- 167, 171: "Biglari" on the class register, "Bahman Biglar" afterwards.
- 169, 199: the mathematics teacher is Mr. Alizadeh, then Mr. Nuralizadeh.
- 181: "that same year 1353", but the paragraph before is set in summer 1354.
- 189: the move to Sakineh's house is dated 1353; "a year and a half" of long
  walks ending in Khordad 1356 points to late 1354.
- 189: Saadi's proverb is quoted with "two dervishes"; Saadi has ten.
- 223–225: "two memories" of Mr. Esmailpur are announced and three are told.

### Chapter 3

- 287: the building is "two single-unit floors", then has two units a floor.
- 291, 293: "Ms. Nowzari" is one sister in one paragraph and the other in the

- 348: dentists rotate "over four years"; the chapter covers about three.
- 374–376: the days around Soheila's birthday are off by one.
- 416: "all of us" got 13 for composition, then "I and some of my friends".

### New in the revised edition

Probable typing slips in the revised Persian. The Persian follows the PDF; the
translations keep the sense the sentence had before, except where noted.

  square as the vocational-studies one became «و», so it now reads "next to
  sciences and vocational studies". The translations keep the old sense, which
  is the one that fits the game.
- 414: «به‌اجبار مجبور» says "forced" twice.
- 556: a stray «آ» after «تتیس», and a full stop inside the bracket: «است.)،».
- 712: «سالی که آغازش … آغاز شد» says "began" twice.
- 764: «همراه بود» was removed, leaving «که با افتضاح سیاسی و موجب … شد»
  without a verb for its first half.
- 796: in the added clause, «با سفارت … اعلام نکرده بود» has «با» where «به»
  is expected, and nothing separates it from «سفارت نیز».
- 964: doubled punctuation at the end, «است.).».
- 966: the rewritten paragraph has almost no punctuation or quotation marks,
  Latin digits (78, 79), «مامور» and «ماموریت» without the hamza, and the
  doubled «گذرگاه عبور عابران مشغول تردد بود».
- 1010: «حل شود» for «حل شد». The translations still say the problem was

- 1044: «جنگ دوم جهانی» here, «جنگ جهانی دوم» elsewhere.
